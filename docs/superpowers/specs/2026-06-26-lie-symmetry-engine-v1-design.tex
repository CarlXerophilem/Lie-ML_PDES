\large A Resumable, Iterative Differential-Elimination Symmetry Engine (v1)}
\author{LieML project --- design note}
\date{2026-06-26}

\begin{abstract}
This document specifies version~1 of the LieML \emph{symmetry engine}: a resumable, iterative
differential-elimination solver that, for a scalar $(1{+}1)$-dimensional PDE of low order,
auto-discovers its Lie point-symmetry algebra, emits residual-zero reductions to ODEs, and saves
\emph{every} outcome --- including ``no symmetry'' --- as a structured, reproducible
\emph{reducibility record}. The engine is the trustworthy symbolic backbone of the wider CCS-SYM+
programme (\texttt{plan.txt}); a locally-trained ``reductor'' and a DeepSeek-V4-Pro ``supervisor''
are designed-for behind stable interfaces but deferred to v1.1. The mathematical core --- the
\emph{defect} and \emph{reducibility index} --- is given precise, implementation-independent
definitions: the discrete part (algebra dimension and type) is an \emph{exact invariant}; the
continuous part (the defect) is a \emph{reproducible diagnostic} relative to a declared parameter
tuple, with the explicit route to a genuine invariant recorded.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}

\paragraph{Purpose.} Move the project from \emph{verifying} hand-supplied Lie generators
(the existing \texttt{symm/feasibility\_demo.py}: \texttt{prolong2}, \texttt{invariance\_residual})
to \emph{automatically solving} the symmetry-determining system and emitting reductions --- the
``S0+S1'' milestone (M1) of \texttt{plan.txt}.

\paragraph{In scope for v1.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Scalar PDEs in two independent variables $(x,t)$ and one dependent variable $u$, of low
        differential order $k$ (target $k\le 3$; validated at $k=2$).
  \item Auto-discovery of the \emph{Lie point}-symmetry algebra by \emph{iterative differential
        elimination} of the determining system (pure \texttt{sympy}; no external CAS dependency).
  \item Residual-zero \emph{reduction} from each discovered generator to a reduced ODE.
  \item A \emph{resumable} checkpoint/resume mechanism: the engine is a state machine that
        checkpoints after every elimination step and reproduces an uninterrupted run exactly.
  \item A \emph{reducibility record} --- the saved outcome of any run, solvable or not, carrying the
        algebra, reductions, a \emph{signature}, a \emph{defect}, a \emph{reducibility index}, and a
        \emph{status} flag.
  \item A polynomial-ansatz \emph{cross-check} that independently computes the algebra dimension.
  \item A beginner-facing \emph{zero-to-one guide}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Out of scope for v1 (designed-for, deferred to v1.1).}
The \emph{learned} reductor encoder; the \emph{live} DeepSeek-V4-Pro supervisor; optimal-system
(conjugacy-class) classification; nonclassical / approximate (BGI) symmetries; systems and
$(2{+}1)$-D PDEs. Each is given a stable interface in v1 so it can be added without rework.

\paragraph{Finish line (the M1 gate).} From scratch the engine must (i)~recover the Burgers dim-$5$
algebra and the porous-medium scaling family, (ii)~emit residual-zero reductions, (iii)~reject a
non-symmetry, and (iv)~resume after an interruption to a \emph{bit-identical} final record.

\section{Background and positioning}

A recent critical synthesis of PDE solvers (\emph{Partial Differential Equations in the Age of
Machine Learning: A Critical Synthesis of Classical, Machine Learning, and Hybrid Methods}, in
\texttt{ref/}; the source of the method-comparison tables in the project notes) catalogues classical,
ML, and hybrid methods across the axes \emph{cost / mesh-adaptivity / UQ / nonlinearity / theory
guarantees}, and argues every family is limited on some axis. Two findings frame this engine.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Exact analytical reduction is absent from that taxonomy.} The only ``Lie'' entry is
        \emph{geometric integrators} (integration \emph{on} a Lie group), not symmetry reduction
        \emph{of} the PDE. Reduction is mesh-free, data-free, dimension-reducing, and provably exact
        --- precisely where numerical/ML methods are weak --- but is narrow: it reaches only
        \emph{special} (group-invariant) solutions, and only when a compatible symmetry exists.
  \item \textbf{The Lie+ML literature is stuck on a single design choice.} A quote-backed reading of
        the core papers shows the bottlenecks all stem from using symmetry as an \emph{ML inductive
        bias}:
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
          \item \emph{Dead training signal} --- point-symmetry losses collapse to $0$ or $c\Delta$
                (Akhound-Sadegh et al., NeurIPS 2023; \emph{Generalized Lie Symmetries in PINOs},
                arXiv:2502.00373, where the naive variant is $\sim 5\times$ worse than a plain PINO).
          \item \emph{Data-proxy ambiguity} --- the same gain is reachable ``by increasing the sample
                size'' (Akhound-Sadegh, stated as a limitation).
          \item \emph{Non-compact groups} --- equivariant operators handle only compact/finite groups
                (G-FNO, arXiv:2306.05697); canonicalization handles arbitrary groups at
                ``$5\text{--}30\times$ slowdown'' with in-distribution regressions (LieLAC,
                arXiv:2410.02698).
          \item \emph{No certificates, toy coverage} --- each paper tests $\sim 2$ PDEs and ships no
                error bound; none automates the prerequisite analytic derivation of the symmetry.
        \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{This engine's position.} Use symmetry as a \emph{reduction / coordinate} device, not an
inductive bias. That single pivot sidesteps the bottleneck cluster (no symmetry-loss term to
collapse; no equivariant layers to pay for; no data), and the engine \emph{automates the one step
all five papers leave manual}. The chosen method --- \emph{iterative differential elimination} --- is
the strongest available tool for that step, and pairs naturally with a reproducible diagnostic that
positions even ``unsolvable'' PDEs rather than discarding them.

\section{Architecture}

The existing flat scripts in \texttt{symm/} remain for demos. The engine is large enough to warrant
focused single-purpose modules in a small package:

\begin{verbatim}
symm/sym_engine/
  jet.py          generic prolongation Dx,Dt,...; prolong(order) for generic xi,tau,phi
  determining.py  split pr^(k)X(Delta)|onshell on free jet monomials -> determining system
  eliminate.py    * the iterative differential-elimination loop (the heart)
  algebra.py      assemble generators from free constants; dim + structure constants + type
  reduce.py       characteristic ODEs dt/tau=dx/xi=du/phi -> invariants z,U -> reduced ODE
  reducibility.py the reducibility record: determining matrix A, signature, defect, index
  checkpoint.py   srepr<->JSON state (de)serialization; save/load latest; step-log writer
  crosscheck.py   polynomial-ansatz linsolve / rational kernel of A -> independent algebra dim
  engine.py       orchestrator: run(pde, resume=True) state machine
  report.py       human-readable run report (feeds the guide)
symm/sym_engine_cli.py     run / resume / inspect a PDE
symm/symmetry_engine.py    thin shim re-exporting the package (keeps plan.txt's filename valid)
symm/tests/test_sym_engine.py
symm/sym_engine/GUIDE_zero_to_one.md
\end{verbatim}

Design principle: each module is independently understandable and testable, communicates through
explicit interfaces, and stays small. \texttt{jet.py} generalises the existing \texttt{prolong2} to
arbitrary infinitesimal functions and to order $k$; everything else is new.

\section{Engine API and data flow}

\begin{verbatim}
from symm.sym_engine import Engine, PDE

pde = PDE.scalar_1plus1(Delta = ut + u*ux - nu*uxx, order=2, name="burgers")
rec = Engine(pde, workdir="symm/runs/burgers").run(resume=True)

rec.dim                # 5
rec.algebra            # generators as (tau, xi, phi) with free-constant labels
rec.reductions         # [(generator, z, U, reduced_ODE, residual==0), ...]
rec.status             # CERTIFIED | PARTIAL | LATENT-ONLY
rec.defect             # delta in [0,1]  (0 when an exact symmetry exists)
rec.reducibility_index # rho in [0,1]
rec.params             # the reproducibility tuple pi (Section 6)
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Flow.}
$\text{PDE} \to \text{build determining system} \to \{\text{iterate: eliminate-step}\to
\text{checkpoint}\} \to \text{solve residual core} \to \text{algebra} \to \text{per-generator
reduction (verify residual-0)} \to \text{reducibility record} \to \text{report}$.
In parallel, \texttt{crosscheck.py} computes the algebra dimension from the rational kernel of the
determining matrix $A$ (Section~\ref{sec:defs}); a disagreement with the elimination result is
flagged as a completeness warning. On stall the run terminates as \texttt{PARTIAL} or
\texttt{LATENT-ONLY} with the \emph{defect} recorded.

\section{Checkpoint and resume}

State is persisted after every elimination step, one directory per run:

\begin{verbatim}
symm/runs/<name>/
  checkpoint-000.json   { determining_eqs, solved_constraints(xi,tau,phi),
  checkpoint-001.json     remaining_unknowns, stage, step_index, params }
  ...                   (sympy expressions stored as srepr strings: exact round-trip)
  steplog.md            human-readable trace of each step (feeds the guide)
  report.md             final report / reducibility record
\end{verbatim}

The per-degree \texttt{checkpoint-NNN.json} files and \texttt{steplog.md} form the audit trail; the
final reducibility record is saved to \texttt{result.json}. In v1, \texttt{run(resume=True)} returns
the saved \texttt{result.json} when the run configuration matches (and the cache is invalidated, with a
logged warning, if it is unreadable or was produced under a different \texttt{d\_star}); otherwise it
recomputes from scratch. Because the engine is \emph{deterministic} (Section~\ref{sec:errors}), that
recompute reproduces the uninterrupted result exactly --- this is an acceptance test, so an interrupted
run yields a bit-identical record either way. (Granular continuation from the highest \emph{per-degree}
checkpoint --- skipping already-computed degrees rather than recomputing --- is a deferred refinement;
the per-degree degrees are cheap, so whole-record caching plus deterministic recompute is the honest v1
realization.) The saved record \emph{is} the reducibility record, so ``save the unsolvable state'' and
``be resumable'' are the same mechanism.

\section{Mathematical definitions and reproducibility contract}
\label{sec:defs}

This section makes the \emph{defect} and \emph{reducibility index} precise enough that two
independent implementations agree. The discrete quantities are exact invariants; only the defect
requires a declared metric.

\paragraph{Setup.} For $\Delta=0$ of order $k$, write the generator
$\X=\tau\,\partial_t+\xi\,\partial_x+\varphi\,\partial_u$. Fix an \textbf{ansatz space} $V_D$ in
which $\tau,\xi,\varphi$ are polynomials in $(x,t,u)$ of total degree $\le D$. With the monomial
basis this gives a coefficient vector $c\in\R^{p}$, where $p=3\binom{D+3}{3}$, and a generator basis
$e_1,\dots,e_p$. The symmetry residual
\[
R(\X)\;:=\;\mathrm{pr}^{(k)}\X(\Delta)\,\big|_{\,\Delta=0\ \text{solved for the
lexicographically-highest derivative}}
\]
is \emph{linear} in $c$. Expanding $R(\X)$ on the distinct monomials $M_\alpha$ in the base and free
jet variables gives $R(\X)=\sum_\alpha P_\alpha(c)\,M_\alpha$; the \emph{determining equations} are
$\{P_\alpha(c)=0\}$.

\begin{definition}[Determining matrix]
\[
A\in\Q^{N\times p},\qquad A_{\alpha i}=\frac{\partial P_\alpha}{\partial c_i},
\]
where $N$ is the number of distinct monomials $M_\alpha$. The entries are exact rationals. This $A$
is also the object used by the polynomial cross-check.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exact part: dimension and type --- invariants]
The polynomial-ansatz symmetry algebra at degree $D$ is $\g=\ker_\Q A$, with
\[
\dim_D \;=\; p-\operatorname{rank}_\Q(A)
\]
computed by \emph{exact rational} rank (no tolerance). With the vector-field bracket the structure
constants of $\g$ yield the derived series $\g^{(1)}=[\g,\g],\g^{(2)},\dots$ and hence the
\textbf{algebra type} (abelian / nilpotent / solvable / semisimple) and the derived-dimension
sequence. $\dim_D$ and the type are \emph{basis-independent invariants}. $\dim_D$ equals the true
point-symmetry dimension whenever all generators are polynomial of degree $\le D$; the
differential-elimination loop is the independent second computation, and the two must agree (the
completeness flag).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Metric part: the defect]
Embed $A$ as a real matrix and fix two symmetric positive-definite inner products: $W_V$ on the
coefficient space $\R^{p}$ and $W_E$ on the equation space $\R^{N}$. Form
\[
\hat A \;=\; W_E^{1/2}\,A\,W_V^{-1/2},\qquad
\boxed{\;\delta \;=\; \frac{\sigma_{\min}(\hat A)}{\sigma_{\max}(\hat A)}\;\in[0,1]\;}
\]
the reciprocal $2$-norm condition number of $\hat A$.
\end{definition}

\noindent This fixes the three quantities the design review flagged:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Optimization domain:} the unit sphere $\{\,c:\|c\|_{W_V}=1\,\}$. Indeed
        $\sigma_{\min}(\hat A)=\min_{\|c\|_{W_V}=1}\|\hat A c\|_{W_E}$ is the residual of the best
        \emph{normalized} near-symmetry generator ($c=0$ is excluded by the constraint).
  \item \textbf{Norm:} the declared $\|\cdot\|_{W_V}$ and $\|\cdot\|_{W_E}$. \emph{Default} $=$
        identity (Euclidean on monomial coefficients). \emph{Recommended} $=$ Bombieri (apolar)
        weights, under which $\delta$ is additionally invariant to orthogonal changes of
        $(x,t,u)$.
  \item \textbf{Normalization:} $\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}$ is scale-free; $\delta$ is invariant
        under $\Delta\mapsto\lambda\Delta$ and $\X\mapsto\mu\X$. Moreover $\delta=0$ if and only if
        an exact symmetry exists (then $\ker\hat A\neq 0$), and $\delta\in(0,1]$ for a barren PDE
        measures the distance to the nearest symmetry.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Reducibility index]
With a declared target depth $d^\ast$ (default $2$ for scalar $(1{+}1)$: one symmetry reduces to an
ODE, a solvable pair to a quadrature),
\[
\rho \;=\;
\begin{cases}
0.5+0.5\,\min\!\big(\dim_D/d^\ast,\ 1\big), & \dim_D\ge 1 \quad(\rho\in[0.5,1],\ \text{reducible}),\\[6pt]
0.5\,(1-\delta), & \dim_D=0 \quad(\rho\in[0,0.5),\ \text{barren}).
\end{cases}
\]
$\rho$ is monotone; the value $0.5$ marks ``has an exact symmetry.'' For reducible PDEs $\rho$ is
\emph{exact} (depends only on the integer $\dim_D$); only the barren branch uses the metric
$\delta$.
\end{definition}

\begin{contract}
Every record carries the parameter tuple
\[
\pi \;=\; \big(\,D,\ \text{on-shell-rule id},\ W_V,\ W_E,\ d^\ast\,\big).
\]
Given $\pi$, two independent implementations compute bit-identical $\dim_D$, algebra type, and
(reducible) $\rho$ over $\Q$, and agree on $\delta$ and the barren $\rho$ to floating-point
tolerance.
\end{contract}

\begin{designnote}[Invariant vs.\ diagnostic --- stated honestly]
$\dim_D$ and the algebra type are \emph{genuine invariants}. The defect $\delta$ (hence the barren
$\rho$) is a \emph{reproducible diagnostic relative to $\pi$} --- not yet a PDE invariant, because it
depends on the ansatz degree and the metric. The route to a true invariant is explicit: use the
Bombieri inner product (removing basis dependence under orthogonal maps) and take the $D\to\infty$
stabilization limit. v1 ships the parameter-tagged diagnostic, with the exact, invariant
$\dim_D$/type as the backbone.
\end{designnote}

\section{The reducibility record}

Every run terminates in a record, never a bare failure:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.26\linewidth} p{0.42\linewidth} p{0.24\linewidth}@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Meaning} & \textbf{Source / status} \\ \hline
\texttt{algebra}, \texttt{dim}, \texttt{reductions} & exact wins (possibly empty) & elimination + reduce; exact \\
\texttt{signature} & $\dim_D$, algebra type, derived-dimension sequence, structure constants & \texttt{algebra.py}; invariant \\
\texttt{defect} ($\delta$) & $\sigma_{\min}(\hat A)/\sigma_{\max}(\hat A)$ & \texttt{reducibility.py}; diagnostic \\
\texttt{reducibility\_index} ($\rho$) & $[0,1]$; $1=$ exact ODE, $0=$ structureless & derived from $\dim_D,\delta$ \\
\texttt{status} & \texttt{CERTIFIED} / \texttt{PARTIAL} / \texttt{LATENT-ONLY} & \texttt{engine.py} \\
\texttt{params} ($\pi$) & the reproducibility tuple & stamped at run end \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\texttt{LATENT-ONLY} is the saved ``unsolvable'' state: $\dim_D=0$ with a recorded defect
and index, available later as labelled training data for the learned reductor.

\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{Secrets discipline (carried from the project).} The DeepSeek key is read from the
environment variable \texttt{DEEPSEEK\_API\_KEY} only --- never hardcoded, never written to a
checkpoint, report, or the guide.

\section{Error handling and determinism}
\label{sec:errors}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Stall detection.} If an elimination step makes no progress for $N$ consecutive
        passes, the run stops as \texttt{PARTIAL}, state saved (resumable; the supervisor stub
        recommends the documented Maple hand-off).
  \item \textbf{Runaway symbolic growth.} A per-run budget (maximum passes, maximum expression size)
        forces a graceful checkpoint-and-stop rather than an unbounded hang.
  \item \textbf{Barren PDE.} $\dim_D=0$ (and defect above a declared threshold) yields
        \texttt{LATENT-ONLY} with the record saved.
  \item \textbf{Determinism.} Determining equations are ordered by a stable complexity key; no
        randomness is used anywhere. Resuming therefore reproduces the uninterrupted result exactly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Testing and the M1 acceptance gate}

\texttt{symm/tests/test\_sym\_engine.py}:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Burgers} $u_t+uu_x=\nu u_{xx}$: recover $\dim=5$ from scratch; the five generators
        match $\partial_t,\partial_x$, Galilean, scaling, projective up to basis; each reduction has
        residual $0$.
  \item \textbf{Porous medium} $u_t=(u^m u_x)_x$: recover the scaling family ($a=2b-me$); the
        Barenblatt reduction has residual $0$.
  \item \textbf{Negative controls}: a non-symmetry generator is rejected (defect $\neq 0$); a PDE
        with no nontrivial symmetry returns \texttt{LATENT-ONLY} with a recorded defect.
  \item \textbf{Resume}: interrupt mid-run, resume, and assert a bit-identical final record.
  \item \textbf{Cross-check}: the rational-kernel dimension of $A$ agrees with the elimination
        result for Burgers.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The zero-to-one guide}

\texttt{symm/sym\_engine/GUIDE\_zero\_to\_one.md}, beginner-facing, building on \texttt{intro.tex}:

\hline
\textbf{Risk} & \textbf{Mitigation} \\ \hline
Hand-rolled elimination stalls on a hard system. & Scope to scalar $(1{+}1)$, low order (M1 already de-risked: a degree-$\le 2$ ansatz gives 28 split equations $\to$ 5-parameter Burgers branch); stall detection $+$ documented Maple hand-off. \\
