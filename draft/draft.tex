\title{\bf Symmetry as Reduction, Not Bias:\\
a certified, resumable symmetry engine and a\\
reducibility index for partial differential equations}
\author{LieML project --- working draft}
\date{2026-06-26}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Modern surveys of PDE solvers compare classical, machine-learning (ML), and hybrid families across
cost, mesh-dependence, uncertainty quantification, nonlinearity, and theory guarantees, and conclude
that every family is limited on some axis. We make two observations. First, \emph{exact analytical
reduction by Lie symmetry is absent from that taxonomy} --- it is mesh-free, data-free,
dimension-reducing, and provably exact, precisely where numerical and ML methods are weak, yet it is
not treated as a solver family. Second, the existing ``Lie${+}$ML'' literature is bottlenecked by a
single design choice: it uses symmetry as an \emph{ML inductive bias} (a loss penalty, an
equivariant architecture, or a canonicalization), which produces dead training signals, non-compact
group costs, and a data-versus-structure ambiguity that the papers themselves concede. We argue for
using symmetry the classical way --- as a \emph{reduction and coordinate device} --- and we
operationalize it as a \emph{resumable, iterative differential-elimination engine} that
auto-discovers a scalar PDE's point-symmetry algebra, emits residual-zero reductions to ODEs, and
saves \emph{every} outcome, including ``no symmetry,'' as a reproducible \emph{reducibility record}.
The record carries an exact, basis-independent dimension and algebra type (genuine invariants) and a
metric \emph{defect} and \emph{reducibility index} (a reproducible diagnostic relative to a declared
parameter tuple). We give precise, implementation-independent definitions of these quantities, place
the engine inside a reduce--close--certify--correct pipeline, and state a falsifiable validation
gate against closed-form similarity solutions. The contribution is a positioning and a precise,
verifiable construction; empirical results are forthcoming.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A partial differential equation (PDE) is hard for two structural reasons: it has many independent
variables, and an enormous solution space. Numerical methods (finite elements, finite volumes,
spectral methods) discretize that space; machine-learning methods (physics-informed networks,
neural operators) regress it. A recent critical synthesis catalogues these families across the axes
\emph{computational cost}, \emph{mesh-adaptivity}, \emph{uncertainty quantification (UQ)},
\emph{nonlinearity handling}, and \emph{theory guarantees}, and argues that the field divides
epistemologically: classical methods are \emph{deductive} (they carry a-priori and a-posteriori
error bounds derived from PDE regularity, but suffer the curse of dimensionality, mesh-generation
cost, and the need to know the physics in advance), while ML methods are \emph{inductive} (flexible
and mesh-free, but lacking certified bounds, data-hungry, and prone to out-of-distribution failure).
Every family is limited on some axis.

This paper starts from a single conspicuous absence in that taxonomy and a single recurring
bottleneck in the literature that tries to repair it.

\paragraph{The missing column.} Classical \emph{analytical reduction} by Lie symmetry --- the
systematic art of using a continuous symmetry to remove an independent variable and collapse a PDE
to an ordinary differential equation (ODE) --- does not appear as a solver family in the survey. The
only ``Lie'' entry concerns geometric integrators, i.e.\ integration \emph{on} a Lie group, not
symmetry reduction \emph{of} the PDE. Yet reduction inverts nearly every weakness in the table: it
is mesh-free, introduces no discretization error, requires no data, reduces dimension, and yields
\emph{provably exact} solutions even for strongly nonlinear equations. Its honest catch is
narrowness: it reaches only \emph{special}, group-invariant solutions, and only when a compatible
symmetry exists.

\paragraph{The recurring bottleneck.} A body of recent work fuses Lie symmetry with ML. Read
carefully, its limitations all stem from one design choice --- treating symmetry as an \emph{ML
inductive bias}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Dead training signal.} Lie-point-symmetry loss terms frequently collapse to $0$ or
        a multiple of the PDE residual, providing no gradient; the proposed evolutionary-symmetry
        repair is stated as a hypothesis and tested on a handful of equations
        \cite{akhound,genlie}.
  \item \textbf{Non-compact groups.} Equivariant operators are built for compact/finite groups
        \cite{gfno}; the PDE-relevant groups (scaling, Galilean, conformal) are non-compact, and the
        one method that handles arbitrary groups pays a $5\text{--}30\times$ inference cost with
        in-distribution regressions \cite{lielac}.
  \item \textbf{Data versus structure.} The same accuracy gains are reachable ``by increasing the
        sample size,'' a point conceded as a limitation \cite{akhound}; whether symmetry is a
        \emph{fundamental} advantage or a \emph{data proxy} is unresolved.
  \item \textbf{No certificates, narrow coverage.} The fusion papers test few PDEs and ship no error
        bound, and crucially \emph{none automates the prerequisite analytic derivation of the
        symmetry}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Thesis.} Use symmetry the classical way. As a \emph{reduction and coordinate device},
symmetry sidesteps the entire bottleneck cluster: there is no symmetry-loss term to collapse, no
equivariant layer to pay for, and no data. What remains is to \emph{automate} the one step every
fusion paper leaves manual --- discovering the symmetry and performing the reduction --- and to be
honest about reduction's own narrowness by \emph{quantifying} it. We contribute:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item a \emph{resumable, iterative differential-elimination engine} that auto-discovers a scalar
        $(1{+}1)$ PDE's Lie point-symmetry algebra and emits residual-zero reductions
        (Section~\ref{sec:engine});
  \item a \emph{reducibility record} that saves every outcome --- including ``unsolvable'' --- as a
        structured object, turning ``no symmetry'' into a \emph{position} rather than a failure
        (Section~\ref{sec:record});
  \item precise, implementation-independent definitions of a \emph{defect} and a \emph{reducibility
        index}, separating exact invariants from a reproducible metric diagnostic
        (Section~\ref{sec:defs});
  \item a falsifiable validation gate against closed-form similarity solutions
        (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background: reduction, and the inductive-bias bottleneck}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Lie symmetry reduction in one paragraph.} A one-parameter symmetry of a PDE is generated
by a vector field $X=\tau\,\partial_t+\xi\,\partial_x+\varphi\,\partial_u$. Prolonging $X$ to act on
derivatives and demanding it preserve the solution set gives the infinitesimal criterion
$\mathrm{pr}^{(k)}X(\Delta)\big|_{\Delta=0}=0$, which, split on the free jet monomials, becomes a
linear, overdetermined \emph{determining system} for $(\tau,\xi,\varphi)$. Its solution space is the
finite-dimensional symmetry algebra $\g$. Each generator supplies invariants --- a similarity
variable $z$ and a dependent invariant --- whose substitution collapses the PDE to an ODE, recovering
travelling-wave and self-similar solutions exactly. The output is closed-form and mesh-free; the
structure is exact, not discretized.

\paragraph{Why the ML fusion stalls.} The bottlenecks of Section~1 are not incidental: they follow
from inserting symmetry into a \emph{statistical} learner. A symmetry-loss penalty is only as useful
as its gradient, which vanishes exactly when the prolonged generator annihilates the residual; an
equivariant architecture must represent the group action in its weights, which is tractable for
compact groups and expensive or impossible otherwise; data augmentation by symmetry is, by
construction, a way to manufacture more samples. Reduction has none of these failure modes because it
never enters the learner: it acts on the \emph{equation}, in closed form, before any model is
trained.

\section{Symmetry as reduction: the engine}
\label{sec:engine}

We realize reduction as a small, auditable engine with one trustworthy rule: \emph{correctness is the
symbolic residual-zero check, never a learned heuristic.}

\paragraph{Iterative differential elimination.} From the generic generator with
$\tau,\xi,\varphi$ polynomial in $(x,t,u)$ of degree $\le D$, the prolongation and the on-shell
substitution (solve $\Delta=0$ for the leading time derivative) produce a residual that is
\emph{linear} in the ansatz coefficients $c$. Splitting it on the free jet monomials yields the
\emph{determining matrix} $A$ over $\Q$. The algebra is $\ker_\Q A$; the engine computes it by exact
rational elimination and \emph{iterates the degree} $D$ until the dimension stabilizes. The
differential content lives in forming the determining system (prolongation and jet-monomial
splitting); the solving is exact elimination. A full step-by-step Rosenfeld--Gr\"obner reduction and
a computer-algebra hand-off remain the documented escape hatch for systems beyond the engine's scope.

\paragraph{Resumability.} The engine is a deterministic state machine that checkpoints after every
degree step, serializing its symbolic state exactly. An interrupted run resumes from the last
checkpoint and reproduces the uninterrupted result bit-for-bit. The same mechanism that makes the
engine resumable also makes the ``unsolvable'' outcome a first-class saved object: the final
checkpoint \emph{is} the reducibility record.

\paragraph{Reduction.} For each discovered generator the engine integrates the characteristic system
$dt/\tau=dx/\xi=du/\varphi$ to obtain invariants, substitutes the resulting ansatz into the PDE, and
\emph{accepts the reduction only if the residual is identically zero}. Translation combinations give
travelling waves; scaling generators give self-similar profiles.

\section{The reducibility record}
\label{sec:record}

Every run terminates in a record, never a bare failure. Beyond the exact algebra and the
residual-zero reductions, the record carries a \emph{signature} (dimension, algebra type, structure
constants), a \emph{defect}, a \emph{reducibility index}, a \emph{status}
(\textsc{certified}/\textsc{partial}/\textsc{latent-only}), and the parameter tuple under which the
metric quantities were computed. A barren PDE is saved as \textsc{latent-only} with a recorded defect
and index --- available later as labelled training data for a learned ranker. ``Unsolvable'' becomes
a coordinate, not a discard. This realizes a deliberate stance: \emph{never label a problem
``unsolved''; position it.}

\section{Definitions: defect and reducibility index}
\label{sec:defs}

We separate what is an exact invariant from what is a reproducible diagnostic.

\paragraph{Setup.} Fix the ansatz degree $D$; the coefficient vector $c\in\R^{p}$,
$p=3\binom{D+3}{3}$, parametrizes $(\tau,\xi,\varphi)$. The on-shell symmetry residual $R(X)$ is
linear in $c$; expanded on the base and free-jet monomials it gives the determining equations and the
matrix $A\in\Q^{N\times p}$ with $A_{\alpha i}=\partial P_\alpha/\partial c_i$.

\begin{definition}[Exact part --- invariants]
$\dim_D=p-\operatorname{rank}_\Q(A)$ (exact rational rank, no tolerance), and the algebra type
(abelian/nilpotent/solvable/semisimple, with the derived-dimension sequence) read off the structure
constants of $\ker_\Q A$. Both are basis-independent invariants.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Metric part --- the defect]
With symmetric positive-definite inner products $W_V$ on $\R^{p}$ and $W_E$ on $\R^{N}$, set
$\hat A=W_E^{1/2}AW_V^{-1/2}$ and
\[
\delta=\frac{\sigma_{\min}(\hat A)}{\sigma_{\max}(\hat A)}\in[0,1],
\]
the reciprocal $2$-norm condition number. Here $\sigma_{\min}(\hat A)=\min_{\|c\|_{W_V}=1}\|\hat A
c\|_{W_E}$ is the residual of the best normalized near-symmetry generator; $\delta=0$ iff an exact
symmetry exists, and $\delta\in(0,1]$ measures distance to the nearest symmetry for a barren PDE.
$\delta$ is invariant under $\Delta\mapsto\lambda\Delta$ and $X\mapsto\mu X$; with the Bombieri inner
product it is additionally invariant under orthogonal changes of variables.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reducibility index]
With target depth $d^\ast$ (default $2$ for scalar $(1{+}1)$),
\[
\rho=
\begin{cases}
0.5+0.5\,\min(\dim_D/d^\ast,1), & \dim_D\ge1 \quad(\text{reducible},\ \rho\in[0.5,1]),\\
0.5\,(1-\delta), & \dim_D=0 \quad(\text{barren},\ \rho\in[0,0.5)).
\end{cases}
\]
The value $0.5$ marks ``has an exact symmetry.'' Reducible $\rho$ is exact; only the barren branch
uses the metric $\delta$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Invariant vs.\ diagnostic, stated honestly]
$\dim_D$ and the algebra type are genuine invariants. The defect $\delta$ (hence the barren $\rho$)
is a \emph{reproducible diagnostic relative to} the declared tuple
$\pi=(D,\ \text{on-shell rule},\ W_V,\ W_E,\ d^\ast)$ --- not yet a PDE invariant, because it depends
on the ansatz and metric. The route to a genuine invariant is explicit: the Bombieri inner product
plus the $D\to\infty$ stabilization limit.
\end{remark}

\section{Pipeline context}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The engine is the front end of a four-stage programme: \textbf{reduce} the PDE by symmetry to an ODE;
\textbf{close} the reduced ODE with a homotopy-analysis series when it is not elementary;
\textbf{certify} the series with a theorem-backed convergence-and-error certificate on the reduced
equation; and \textbf{correct} the remainder with a small symmetry-invariant network expressed in
invariant coordinates. Working in invariant coordinates is what makes the network equivariant by
construction --- the dead-signal pathology of the inductive-bias approaches is \emph{structurally
absent}, not penalized. The reducibility record is the contract between the front end and the rest of
the pipeline: \textsc{certified} hands a clean ODE downstream, \textsc{latent-only} flags an
out-of-scope problem honestly.

\section{Validation plan}
\label{sec:validation}

We adopt a falsifiable gate (``M1''): from scratch, the engine must recover the Burgers
five-dimensional point-symmetry algebra and the porous-medium scaling family, emit residual-zero
reductions, reject a non-symmetry, and resume after an interruption to a bit-identical record. An
independent numerical cross-check (singular-value nullity of $\hat A$) must agree with the exact
rational dimension; a disagreement is reported as a completeness flag rather than hidden. All claims
are validated against \emph{closed-form} oracles --- the Cole--Hopf tanh kink for Burgers, the
Barenblatt profile for the porous medium, the Ablowitz--Zeppetella front for Fisher--KPP --- never
against self-generated numerical reference solutions, following the strong-baseline discipline urged
by recent critiques of the ML-for-PDE literature. Empirical results are reported in a companion
once the engine passes the gate.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Symmetry methods for differential equations are classical \cite{olver,hydon}; differential
elimination for determining systems is well developed \cite{boulier}. The ML side spans
data-augmentation \cite{lpsda}, symmetry-loss penalties \cite{akhound,genlie}, equivariant operators
\cite{gfno}, and canonicalization \cite{lielac}; we have argued these share an inductive-bias
bottleneck. Solvers that build exact symmetry solutions and fit only the data --- closest in spirit
to this work --- exist for linear homogeneous PDEs \cite{liesolver}, but require a seed solution and
do not automate symmetry discovery or quantify reducibility. To our knowledge, packaging a
\emph{reducibility index} that positions any PDE on a continuum from exactly reducible to
structureless, with an exact invariant backbone, is new.

\section{Limitations and outlook}
\label{sec:limits}

The engine is scoped to scalar $(1{+}1)$ PDEs of low order and \emph{point} symmetries; nonclassical
and approximate (Baikov--Gazizov--Ibragimov) symmetries, optimal-system classification, and
$(2{+}1)$-D systems are future work behind stable interfaces. The differential-elimination loop is a
degree-iterated linear elimination, not a full functional Rosenfeld--Gr\"obner reduction; the latter,
with a computer-algebra hand-off, is the escape hatch for harder systems. The defect is, for now, a
parameter-tagged diagnostic rather than a PDE invariant. None of these undercut the central claim:
reduction is the deductive, mesh-free, data-free column the PDE-solver taxonomy is missing, and it is
strongest exactly where the inductive-bias approaches are weakest --- provided we automate it and
quantify, rather than hide, the slice on which it is silent.
