Initial bed constraints

Base: y = 0 · surface: f(x,y) = 0

The two curves bound the sediment region; they are not simultaneous equalities at every bed point. The upper root in 0 ≤ y ≤ 5 m defines the bed. Where no root exists, the base y=0 is used. The profile is extruded across canal width s. Applying it resets time and keeps the initial water-surface elevation where possible.

Use x, y, + − * / ^, parentheses, sin, cos, exp, sqrt, abs, min, max. An equation such as y=0 && y-0.5=0 is also accepted as base + surface.

The physics and numerical method

This is a two-horizontal-dimensional, depth-averaged shallow-water model. It evolves water depth h and momentum hu, hv on an 80 m × 20 m canal, with gravity g = 9.81 m/s². The bed evolves through sediment-flux divergence (Exner equation). Horizontal coordinates are (x,s); y is vertical elevation for the geometry editor. Free-surface waves, shocks, flow around topography, reflection and wetting/drying emerge from the equations.

hₜ + (hu)ₓ + (hv)ₛ = 0
(hu)ₜ + (hu² + ½gh²)ₓ + (huv)ₛ = −ghBₓ − Dₓ
(hv)ₜ + (huv)ₓ + (hv² + ½gh²)ₛ = −ghBₛ − Dᵧ
D = gn² |(u,v)| (hu,hv) / h⁴ᐟ³
(1−p)Bₜ + ∂ₓqᵦₓ + ∂ₛqᵦₛ = 0
qᵦ = A𝗀 |(u,v)|² (u,v) · min(1,h/0.01)²

Solver: 128 × 32 finite-volume cells, local Rusanov flux, hydrostatic reconstruction with bed-pressure corrections, adaptive CFL time step, and semi-implicit Manning drag. A still lake over uneven ground remains still to floating-point accuracy. Dry cells have zero momentum. A first-order split Exner step transports sediment through shared, upwind face fluxes; a donor-volume limiter prevents excavation below the substrate. Water depth and momentum remain conserved during this bed step; the changed bed feeds back into the next water step. This weak-coupling demonstrator restricts A𝗀 ≤ 0.001 and uses an extra coupling-based time-step margin. It is not a fully coupled characteristic solver.

Volume balance compares current water with initial water plus integrated boundary flow. Numerical diffusion smooths sharp fronts; this is a first-order educational solver, not a calibrated flood-engineering tool. Hydrostatic, shallow-water assumptions exclude vertical jets, splashes, overturning waves and suspended sediment or bank collapse.

Bed treatment follows Audusse et al., hydrostatic reconstruction (2004). The cubic Grass bedload law is empirical and must be calibrated to real sediment; see Berthon et al., shallow-water–Exner benchmarks. Transport tapers continuously below 1 cm water depth and vanishes in dry cells. Closed boundaries have zero sediment flux; open ends allow sediment outflow with no sediment supply from outside. The base is y=0; imported or painted negative initial beds use their initial elevation as local substrate. No slope avalanching or suspended-load exchange is included. Painting defines a new initial condition, not an external force during a run. Water surface elevation and depth are different: η = B + h.
