# Week 1 Interim Plan

## Project scope and research position

The week-one target is a verifiable Lie reduction and perturbation study of the published scalar, 1+1-dimensional Lagrangian SVE master equation. The exact `m=3` case is the anchor; `m=3+δ` is the first perturbation. The neural component is a parameter-conditioned correction in invariant coordinates, evaluated against converged conventional solvers. No universal ML advantage is assumed.

"Decoherence" must retain its quantum meaning: loss of off-diagonal coherence in an open quantum state after environmental degrees of freedom are traced out. Relevant work separates into three strands: Lindblad analyses of continuous symmetries and conserved quantities under dissipation; a 2025 Caldeira--Leggett derivation of transient viscous hydrodynamics from a near-diagonal density-matrix expansion and moment closure; and a 2026 perturbative/discrete CP-CPT symmetry treatment of Lindblad dephasing. None derives classical water-wave or SVE equations, and these quantum-generator symmetries are not Lie point symmetries of the engineering PDE. [Albert & Jiang (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.89.022118), [Zhou et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06618), [Klimenko (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjc/s10052-026-15689-x).

For water-based engineering, keep two scale-bridges distinct:

1. **Classical water and sediment:** a real macro-to-meso route is vertical averaging or moment projection of a water-sediment Navier--Stokes model, coupled to suspended-load concentration and an Exner bed-load mass balance with erosion/deposition closure. Recent HSWEMED work demonstrates this sequence and retains vertical velocity moments; it is an engineering scale reduction, not quantum decoherence. For grain-resolved microphysics, particle/kinetic closure would be a still finer, separate extension. [Parvin et al. (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22278).
2. **Quantum open-fluid extension:** derive a completely positive, trace-preserving Lindblad or Caldeira--Leggett model; preserve density-matrix positivity and trace while introducing any time-dependent dephasing rate; reduce it to density-matrix or Wigner-moment dynamics; close the hierarchy; then classify symmetries of the derived hydrodynamic PDE. This is a possible separate project, not the current SVE model.

The supplied *Finite Time Blowup for Navier--Stokes* PDF is useful only as a construction-design analogy: it stages profile corrections to cancel residual terms. It does not supply evidence for SVE physics, decoherence, Lie symmetry, or PINN performance.

The supplied 2026 Lagrangian SVE paper already provides the target point-symmetry classification and similarity-reduction reference; an earlier one-dimensional Eulerian SVE Lie analysis also exists. The novelty claim must therefore exclude “first Lie analysis of SVE,” discovery of (X_5), or simply “Lie symmetry plus PINN.” A 2026 Nature Communications method learns time-dependent moving/rescaled frames and solves symmetry-coupled index-2 DAEs for nonlinear examples, while LieSolver (2026 preprint) constructs PDE solutions from known symmetries for linear homogeneous PDEs. The proposed distinction is independent verification, exceptional-exponent perturbation/obstruction analysis, and controlled parameter-conditioned correction to the reduced analytical profile. [Paliathanasis et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3390/math14030433), [Earlier SVE Lie analysis (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym14081679), [Kavousanakis et al. ## Machine-learning sockets

| Socket | Formulation and role | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduced analytical remainder** | Primary method: `F̂θ = F_an + W Nθ(σ; μ)`, where `W` enforces known endpoint/Taylor constraints and `μ` contains admissible physical parameters. Train on the reduced residual, exact boundary constraints, and optional observation mismatch. | Week-one design; later training after reduction and reference tests pass. |
| **True post-PINN correction** | Train a baseline PINN, freeze it, then learn `Cθ` in `F_PINN + W Cθ` against the remaining residual. Treat as an ablation distinct from the analytical-remainder model. | Optional comparison if the reduced baseline PINN is stable. |
| **Parameterized operator/surrogate** | Learn a map from reduced input functions or a low-dimensional coefficient vector to profiles when many inflow, forcing or parameter cases must be queried. This amortizes repeated solves; it is unnecessary for a single profile. | Later family-scale extension. |
| **Conservative neural correction** | For a future Eulerian SVE system with fronts or shocks, discretize fluxes conservatively (e.g., finite-volume/PINN hybrid) and correct flux/residual defects instead of relying only on pointwise strong-form residuals. A 2026 FFV-PINN preprint combines simplified finite-volume convection with residual correction for fluid PDEs. | Later full-system extension; not interchangeable with the scalar reduced equation. [Wei et al. (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24114). |
| **Inverse/data and uncertainty tools** | Add sparse observations to estimate unknown coefficients; use ensembles or residual-adaptive sampling to flag uncertain/high-defect regions. Keep held-out parameters and independent PDE checks. | Optional, after forward benchmark. |
| **Quantum open-system surrogate** | Only for a separate quantum-fluid study: use a positive, trace-one density-matrix parameterization or CPTP evolution model, then learn the map from bath/dephasing parameters to Wigner moments or transport coefficients. | Separate project; not the SVE week-one model. |

Required comparison set: analytical truncation alone; hybrid reduced profile; conventional adaptive reduced ODE solver as reference; reduced PINN without the analytical component; direct PDE PINN; and symmetry-loss PINN. Add the moving-frame PINN only if it can solve the same admissible data problem. Report profile and reconstructed-field errors, the original PDE residual, positivity margin, held-out parameter accuracy, data mismatch when present, inference/training cost, and variation across seeds. Neural wins and losses are both publishable outcomes.

### Suggested interim-report summary

This week establishes a checkable route from the published scalar Lagrangian SVE equation to a symmetry-reduced benchmark and a falsifiable learning study. Work first reproduces the exceptional `m=3` reduction, then tests whether that structure persists under constant-exponent and controlled coefficient perturbations. Decoherence-informed quantum hydrodynamics is retained as a separate long-term micro-to-macro motivation; the current classical SVE study uses sediment and bed-load closures instead. The proposed ML contribution is a parameter-conditioned post-reduction analytical remainder, with a true post-PINN defect corrector and operator/conservative models treated as controlled extensions. No accuracy or speed advantage is claimed before independent references and reconstruction checks are complete.
