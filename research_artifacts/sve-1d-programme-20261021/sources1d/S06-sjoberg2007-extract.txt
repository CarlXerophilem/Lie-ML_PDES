Abstract

   We use the deﬁnition of the association of symmetries of partial diﬀerential equations with conservation laws [A. Kara,
F. Mahomed, The relationship between symmetries and conservation laws, Int. J. Theor. Phys. 39 (1) (2000) 23–40] to ﬁnd
a double reduction of partial diﬀerential equations with two independent variables. Applications to the linear heat equa-
tion, the BBM equation, the sine-Gordon equation and a system of diﬀerential equations from one dimensional gas
dynamics illustrate the new theory.

Keywords: Partial diﬀerential equations; Lie point symmetries; Conservation laws; Double reduction; Association; Heat equation; BBM
equation; sine-Gordon equation; One dimensional gas dynamics




1. Introduction

   It is well known that when a diﬀerential equation admits a Noether symmetry, a conservation law is asso-
ciated with this symmetry, and furthermore that a double reduction can be achieved as a result of this asso-
ciation [2–4]. The association of conservation laws with Noether symmetries was extended to Lie-Bäcklund
symmetries [1] and non-local symmetries [5–7] recently. This opened the door to the extension of the theory
on double reductions to partial diﬀerential equations (PDEs) that do not have a Lagrangian and therefore do
not possess Noether symmetries.
   In this article we develop a theorem to eﬀect a double reduction of PDEs with two independent variables.
Such a double reduction is possible when a PDE (or system of PDEs) admits a symmetry which is associated
with a conservation law.
   The next section contains the notation and some known results that is used in the development of the new
theory in Section 3. In Section 4 the theory is demonstrated on three scalar PDEs (the heat equation, the sine-
Gordon equation and the BBM equation) and a system of PDEs from one dimensional gas dynamics.

5. Discussion

   In this article we have developed new theory that explains how a symmetry and its associated conservation
law can be used to do a double reduction of partial diﬀerential equations with two independent variables.
   A study is needed on if/how the second reduction, due to the association with a conservation law, is con-
nected to a symmetry reduction.
   The generalization of this theory to PDEs of higher dimensions is still an open problem and is intimately
connected with the deﬁnition of non-local variables (see [3,4,6]). The deﬁnition of these variables are known in
theory, but it is not clear how to overcome the problem that not all derivatives of the non-local variables are
known explicitly. Calculations for higher dimensions become quite involved. One only has to consider the lack
of examples of potential symmetries and symmetries with associated conservation laws for higher dimensional
PDEs to demonstrate the complexity of this problem.
   The examples given demonstrate the use of the theory to reduce both scalar PDEs and systems of PDEs
with two independent variables.
